# mg-prop-hunt-maps

The maps mg-prop-hunt plays, as JSON documents. Read `../../CLAUDE.md` and `../mg-prop-hunt/CLAUDE.md` first: the format is defined and validated by mg-prop-hunt's `game/ph_map_doc.gd`, and `maps/README.md` is the mapper's copy of it.

**`maps/` is what the game reads**, through a dot-bootstrap link (`mg-prop-hunt/.gitignore` names it: `# bootstrap-link: mg-prop-hunt-maps/maps`). The game reads every `.json` there plus the practice house built into it; a refused document is listed by `ph_maps` with the reason, and every other map still plays.

**`tools/build_maps.py` writes every document, and the JSON is committed.** Edit the builder, run it, commit both. `--check` (CI) fails when a committed file differs from what the builder writes. The builder exists because a building is hundreds of boxes that have to meet: a wall with a door in it is five boxes whose edges are the door's, and a classroom is thirty desks and chairs in rows.

**Prop sizes are a snapshot** (`tools/prop_sizes.json`), copied from mg-prop-hunt's `props/catalogue.json` by `--sync-sizes`, so the builder (and CI) runs without the game checked out. The builder needs a prop's size to stand it against a wall, on a table, or to keep a spawn clear of it; a prop it does not know stops the build. **`on()` rests a prop on what is under its footprint**, from the support's `tops` grid (copied from the game's `props/pieces.json`, its part-shaped collision): a bed's mattress, not its headboard's height; a corner desk's notch is floor, so put things on its arms. Re-run `--sync-sizes` after the game re-measures its props.

## Things the builder decides for you

- **Spawns are settled** (`Map._settle_spawns`): a spawn is written where a room's plan says players start, before the room is furnished, and a sofa put down later lands on it. mg-prop-hunt's `headless_maps` found six that way; the builder now moves each blocked one to the nearest clear, floored spot in rings of half a metre and prints what it moved. A spawn with nothing clear within four metres stops the build.
- **Nothing stands in a doorway or out in a hallway** (`Map._check_layout`): a hole down to the floor in a `wall()` (4 m wide or less) is a doorway, kept clear of floor props for 1 m either side and of solid boxes for 0.5 m below 2 m; a `room(..., hall=True)` keeps its props within 0.9 m of its walls. Either stops the build and names the prop. Christian's first play test found a sink in the school's hallway; the check then found a toilet inside each bathroom's door, the north classrooms' boards across their doors at chest height, a kitchen counter, a television, a bathtub and a fence across doors on the house, and the woods' garden gate onto the melons. Rugs (under 0.1 m) and props on tables do not count. mg-prop-hunt's `headless_bots` also holds every hiding place reachable from the props' start.
- **Things belong where they are**: lunch (plates, a drink, fruit) on the cafeteria's tables, water bottles on desks and gym benches, pots and knife blocks on the kitchens' counters, crates, barrels and buckets in store rooms and the garage, a camp in the woods. A prop hunt's props hide as what is in the room, so a room's dressing is what a player can be there. Small pieces stand on others with `on()`.
- **A classroom's board goes on the wall away from its door** (`classroom(..., board_side=)`, "n" or "s"; the "s" room is the "n" room mirrored), and no window runs behind a board.
- **Roof eaves are per room** (`room(..., eave=)`): a roof reaches 0.3 m past its room to cover the walls and overhang the outside, but a room beside a taller one (the school's hallway and bathroom beside the gym) wants `WALL / 2`, or its roof shows as a dark band inside the taller room. Found by rendering the gym.
- **A sunken thing is cut out of the ground** (`floor_around`): the house's pool and the woods' pond sit below a lawn that would otherwise be drawn over them. Found by rendering: the first house had a pool of grass.
- **A tall room is lined on the inside** (the school's gym): brick above a painted wall read as a missing wall.

## Toys

`TOY_PLANS` puts a few of dot-props' **loose** toys on each map (`Map.toy`, settled at build by `_settle_toys` to the nearest spot within `near` that is floored, clear of every prop, solid box, spawn and other toy, and a metre from every doorway; a hint with nowhere stops the build). Never a fixed toy: it would be cover or a way up to a hiding place, and nobody can hide as one. The code is game-hide-n-seek-maps' with the doorway check in this builder's own terms. mg-prop-hunt's `headless_maps` checks each is built, inside nothing, and still two seconds on.

## Each map is a close game on its own

mg-prop-hunt's `headless_bots` holds every map to its own band (the props win 25-65% of its rounds at medium, 12 rounds a map), not only the four together. With the server's one hunter in three the stand-ins gave the props 11 rounds in 12 on the school (319 hiding places, every one among others of its kind), 7 on the office, 2 in the house and 1 in the woods (68 and 101 places, fewer of each kind). Each map now sets its own: the school `players_per_hunter=1.6` (five hunters for eight; four gave the props 36 of its own 48 seeds) and a 20 s hide; the house and the woods 4.0 (two hunters), the woods a 240 s round; the office the server's. Measured 2026-10-12 over 48 rounds: school 7 of 12, house 5, office 7, woods 5. Re-measure (`headless_bots -- --map <id>`, 48 of that map's rounds) after dressing a map: more pieces of a kind is more places to be lost among. A one-round change is noise: any body added to the physics space, a toy or an invisible box, re-rolls a knife-edge round (mg-prop-hunt docs/history.md).

**The ported maps are tuned the same way (2026-10-20, 24 rounds a map with `headless_bots -- --map <id>`).** Three things learned: a map's `round_seconds` and `hide_seconds` can only lower the server's (300 and 30), never raise them, so a map that is too hard on the hunters has only its hunter share to turn; the share moves in whole hunters (eight players: 3 hunters at 3.0, 2 at 4.0, 4 at 2.0, 5 at 1.6, 6 at 1.4), and a map's rate can jump from 5% to 80% across one step, so a shorter round is the fine knob (the hotel: 3 hunters, 240 s); and a map whose stairs or gates leave most of its hiding places cut off from the props' start (`ph_frontyard` 9 of 41 reachable, `ph_restaurant_os`) cannot be balanced by any of these, only by linking the nav in the importer. `ph_autumngetaway` jumps from every round to none between four and five hunters because props stand stuck in terrain pits.

**Why three ported maps had bots that could not reach their places, and what the importer does about it (2026-10-20).** A map document has no ladder and no link, so a place is reachable only if the walkable floor is joined by shared mesh edges. Four causes, each fixed by a general rule rather than a per-map hack: door trim posts and door leaves left in doorways (`drop_trim_posts`, `drop_door_leaves`); doorways of 0.75 to 1.1 m in thick or vertically sliced walls (`--widen-thick-doors`, `widen_sliced_doors`, which also clears a sill piece in the gap); ladder-only levels (`--prune-levels` drops props below or well above the modelled floor, and with it a slab up to the mesh's 0.4 m climb counts as floor and a prop spawn in a sealed room is dropped); and pocket pruning silently doing nothing on terrain maps (`--terrain` lowers the floor limit, `--pocket-margin=0.07` widens the erosion). Tuned over 24 rounds each: `ph_frontyard` 2.7 players per hunter and 180 s rounds (11 of 24 props win; only the ground floor is modelled, so 56 props; the melon patch is fenced off and is pruned), `ph_restaurant_os` 4.0 per hunter (54%), `ph_autumngetaway` 1.6 per hunter and 60 s rounds (58%). `tools/ports.txt` holds each map's flags.

**`tools/reimport.py` now reproduces the cut-off fixes (2026-10-23); run it as `heavy -- tools/reimport.py <id>`.** After the import it puts the author's environment back (`environments.sh`'s line), then settles the map: a bot round (`PH_BOTS_TRACE=1 ... headless_bots -- --map <id> --rounds 1`) names each place cut off from the props' start and `retune.py --drop-beside` drops the prop beside it, until none is left, then drops what the game's own `headless_maps` names as sunk or floating (`game_audit`), repeating the two until the audit drops nothing; when over a tenth of the places are cut off it first widens every doorway (`retune.py --widen-thick-doors`). A map whose settling drops over 12% of its props is put back as committed. `retune.py <id> [--widen-doors] [--carve=x0,z0,x1,z1] [--drop-beside=x,z,kind] ...` still edits a committed document in place; `tools/test_retune.py` covers carve, widen-doors, idempotence, the no-op rule and reimport's parsing. Keep a re-import only when its `headless_bots -- --map <id> --rounds 24` checks are no worse than the committed one's. A fresh import is a different map (the shape matcher re-sizes the props), so its balance (`players_per_hunter`) is untuned: only `ph_westerncity` is kept (17/1 against 17/1, props 11 of 24, 0 of 1416 places cut off, 5895 boxes). Rejected, checks passed/failed committed -> settled re-import: `ph_gas_station` 16/2 -> 15/3 (props 18 of 24), `ph_swimmingpool` 16/2 -> 11/7 (props 3), `ph_midwestapartment` 17/1 -> 15/3, `ph_volcano` 15/3 -> 14/4, `ph_restaurant_os` 16/2 -> 15/3 (props 15), `ph_oceanwaves` 17/1 -> 16/2, `ph_islandhouse` 16/2 -> 16/2 (props 6), `ph_bikinibottom` 14/4 -> 13/5 (props 1 of 24); each needs its hunter share tuned before a re-import is worth keeping. `ph_bikinibottom` used to crash the importer: its sea bed is displacement terrain and ports.txt had no `--terrain`, so every start was over nothing and dropped. The importer now turns terrain on itself when most starts have no box under them and the map has displacements, coarsens the field to stay under the 6000-box limit, and stops with a message instead of an IndexError when no start is floored.

**Unmapped models and the shape matcher (2026-10-23).** Unmapped models are about 8 to 20% of each map's props (lights, sky models, the author's small items) except `ph_cyber_dinner` (57% mapped): its models are in the .bsp's embedded pakfile (lump 40, 111 mdl/vvd/vtx), not shipped with the workshop addon. dot-map's `dotbsp.models` measures them and `import_bsp.py` uses the measured size: a name guess from `PROP_MAP` stands only within `GUESS_LIMIT` of the model's size, otherwise the catalogue prop of the nearest shape within `SHAPE_LIMIT` does, and scenery over 3 m is dropped (2026-10-23: unplaced prop entities 565 -> 63). The cut-off fixes of 2026-10-23 (westerncity 96 of 1680 places cut off, bikinibottom 24/576, gas_station 24/792, oceanwaves 24/552, abandoned_hotel 48/2160) were first made by hand with retune.py and are now what settling does.

**Re-importing the other pakfile maps with the shape matcher (2026-10-23, 24 rounds each, checks passed/failed before -> after): only `ph_bank` improved (16/2 -> 17/1, props 14 -> 12 of 24) and was kept.** `ph_volcano` 15/3 -> 14/4 (props 7; at 2.0 per hunter 13/5, props 16), `ph_westerncity` 17/1 -> 13/5 (864 of 1128 places cut off, props 24), `ph_gas_station` 16/2 -> 15/3 (528 of 1248 cut off), `ph_swimmingpool` 16/2 -> 10/8, `ph_restaurant_os` 16/2 -> 15/3, `ph_oceanwaves` 17/1 -> 16/2, `ph_midwestapartment` 17/1 -> 12/6, `ph_islandhouse` 16/2 -> 14/4 all stayed as committed: a fresh import throws away the hand-made cut-off fixes in ports.txt (reimport.py now repeats them by settling). `ph_bikinibottom` still crashes the importer (every start is dropped as over nothing: no floor box under them). The other five have no pakfile models (`ph_abandoned_hotel`, `ph_elementary_school`, `ph_autumngetaway`) or no source on this machine (`ph_frontyard`, `ph_wolfsmanor`), so the matcher changes nothing for them.

**`prop_static` is set dressing, not a hiding place, and the importer leaves it out on purpose.** It is compiled into the `sprp` game lump (dot-map's `static_props()`), is fixed in the author's map (nobody disguises as it in the original), and is 4 to 40% of the movable props' number per map; turning it into disguise targets would add hundreds of places that never existed and re-roll every balance above. Its collision as cover is not carried either; add it to the importer as boxes only if a map shows players walking through fixtures.

## Lighting

Outdoor maps are lit by the sun and the ambient colour in the document's `environment`. The first renders were washed out (sun 1.15, ambient 0.6 on the school); 0.95 and 0.42 read as daylight. The woods' fog was 0.008 and turned the whole map orange from above; it is 0.0025. Render after changing either: `mg-prop-hunt/tools/shot.sh --view=overview --ph-map-ids=<id>`.

**The converted maps carry the author's own environment (2026-10-20).** `tools/bsp_environment.py <map.bsp> maps/<id>.json` patches a finished document, touching nothing else (a re-import would re-roll the pruning and the balance tuned above): the first `light_environment` becomes the sun (colour, brightness, angle) and the shade's colour, the `env_fog_controller` becomes `fog_colour/start/end/max` in metres (never ending nearer than 160 m nor denser than 0.7, so a hunter sees a prop across a street), the sky's name only picks a mood to paint (the author's sky is never carried), and `point_spotlight` and `env_fire` become lamps the importer dropped (kept 2.5 m from any light there; the author's own are thinned to stay within 128). A map the author lit with lamps alone (`ph_abandoned_hotel`, `ph_frontyard`) has no sun to read and keeps its environment. None of the sources has a `func_precipitation`, so there is no weather and the game links no dot-weather; when a map has one, read it as murder-mystery-maps' importer does. The same code is in murder-mystery-maps' importer: it belongs in dot-map's shared reader (nightly `dotbsp-environment`), and this file is the copy to delete when it moves. `tools/environments.sh` runs it over every map with its sound beds, once on a fresh import.

## Publishing

**This repository is a pack of its own, mounted on the server alone.** mg-prop-hunt's `game.yml` names it under `server_dependencies`; a server mounts it and the game adds that mount's `maps/` to its catalogue (`PhModule._add_delivered_maps`). It is never in a client's content sync: a client is sent the one document being played. `.github/workflows/release.yml` runs `build_maps.py --check` and then dot-ci's release with `pack: true` (and `server-compat: false`, because there is no code in it to check). dot-server-deploy's `content/prophunt_maps/` and `examples/prophunt_client` (22 checks) prove the delivered pair works over a real socket.

`project.godot` exists only so dot-ci's release can import this repository and attach the `-pack.zip`; it carries the editor's own header and no comments, because the editor rewrites the file on its first save and a checkout would show it modified.

**Not yet on GitHub** (2026-10-09): the remote is set, the repository is the owner's to create, and nothing is pushed or published.

**`provides.json` is what lists this pack in the Workshop, and `tools/build_provides.py` writes it** from the documents in the folder (an entry per map, `map_kind` and the game it is for are constants at the top). Edit the script, run it, commit both; `--check` (CI) fails on a stale file. A pack whose newest READY version has no `provides` block is not a Workshop item. It ships because the pack takes every file outside the excluded folders.
